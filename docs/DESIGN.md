# Shidan’s Adventure — design foundation

The basement starts locked each round. Completed consumption rewards reaching or crossing 200 happiness remove its entrance fence and stairwell extensions, with a seven-second popup independent of snack notifications. The milestone persists through catches, home returns and menus. Restarting resets it. The fence uses live collision for Shidan, loose items, poop balls and physical reach; human navigation updates its existing graph's connectivity only when the lock changes.

## Magical basement — frosted-glass maze

Maze ceiling lamps are placed at six shared grid-cell centers (columns 2, 7 and 13; rows 2 and 9). The selected layout supplies their positions before house geometry is batched. All five designs share the same expanded grid, so switching designs needs no live fixture relocation. Shades, mounts, stems, physical boxes, instanced diffusers and the day/night light metadata use the same resolved coordinates. Full-height walls remain in place; no lamp geometry intersects their footprints.

When the selected content is a maze, its floor and ceiling underside are painted sky blue. Per-face vertex colours paint only the bottom of the ground-floor slabs, retaining their existing upper surfaces and physical opening. Utility/future non-maze content retains its own neutral floor/ceiling palette. The tint survives ordinary static batching without overlay geometry or extra colliders.

Ordinary mistress chores select above-ground destinations and paths with a minimum height of zero. The routine planner rejects below-ground waypoints even from navigation adapters. Chase, search and cleanup retain unrestricted basement routing; after an investigation, the separate return phase exits the basement before restoring normal chores.

The first magical scene replaces the utility equipment with a blue frosted-glass maze inside the basement's existing 48 × 40 m shell. A fresh game selects one of five fixed designs, each with 192 connected cells in a 16 × 12 grid with 2.6 × 3.05 m cell spacing, meaningful dead ends, alternate approaches and an open two-by-two-cell heart chamber. Four maze gates lead onto a perimeter circulation route reached from the house's single basement staircase. The maze now occupies x −19.3…22.3, z −18.3…18.3: a 41.6 × 36.6 m footprint, 26.9% larger than its previous 40 × 30 m area. Its outer edges leave 1.48 m clear circulation on the north, east and south; a 1.54 m walking lane remains beside the west stair cheek. The existing staircase stays clear. Four distinct center doorways connect the 5.2 × 6.1 m heart chamber to the corridors. Each maze entrance has at least two internally vertex-disjoint approaches to that chamber, preventing a single outside bottleneck from controlling every route. The concrete floor, ceiling and day/night lighting remain. Home returns, catches and menus preserve the selected scene; new games choose another layout and rebuild human navigation once. The four surrounding basement walls are sky blue.

`basement-scenes.js` first selects the basement content type; only the maze type is registered for now. It then selects a design through `maze-layout-catalog.js`, excluding the immediately preceding design. The five fixed designs are Willow Paths, Ribbon Switchbacks, Clockwork Spiral, Twin Courtyards and Four Winds. Their walls and entrances are stable across repeated loads, while consumable selection remains random. Each design uses a distinct carving profile and fixed seed; `mirror-maze-layout.js` retains the lower-level generator and connected supply/entrance metadata for diagnostics. `mirror-maze.js` owns full-height nonpushable wall colliders, frosted privacy-glass panes and structural frames. The panes use 8% transparency (92% opacity), high roughness and fine procedural grain. They remain solid and block direct sight and physical reach despite their faint visual translucency. The mistress uses ordinary hearing, pursuit, searching, capture and cleanup throughout the maze. The carpet and plush are reachable, without den protection or capture immunity.

`maze-supplies.js` maintains 10–20 randomly placed one-use consumables, independently of household item counts and regeneration. Even bonus cake, wire, cardboard and slippers never regenerate. The central carrot is a flat drawing on the yellow rug, not an additional edible item or food collider. Four fixed maze fountains supplement the fourteen above ground, each with its own 100% reserve. Central special hay repeatedly refills energy to 100%; its three-ball reward is available once per carpet visit, including the first arrival, and renews only after leaving and landing back on the carpet.

The original blue rabbit plush at the heart uses `maze-affection.js` and `maze-heart.js`. Its visible height is approximately 0.78 m versus Shidan’s 1.5 m, with a matching fixed collider. Hold left-click beside it for five uninterrupted seconds to animate kissing and award 500 happiness once per entire round. The heart effect emits 24 particles per second, keeping about 40 visible during a continuous kiss with a hard cap of 64 in one instanced draw. Integer emission indices make the density independent of frame rate and skip expired backlog after delayed frames. Releasing, leaving reach, jumping, opening menus, peeing or capture cancels incomplete progress; another attempt starts from zero. Only completed interaction consumes the reward. A live kiss emits globally audible Shidan noise, masked only by the mistress’s own current noisy episode, so it can interrupt her ordinary routine or searching. It does not replenish energy or carried balls.

The maze wall material is nonreflective blue frosted privacy glass: 92% opacity gives 8% transparency, with fine local procedural grain. An unlit blue finish gives both faces the same blue tint instead of letting opposing warm/cool lights turn one side yellow. Rendering uses ordinary glazing and frame geometry, with no mirrored cameras, scene-capture textures, reflection cache or per-frame reflection passes. Pane collision and the human navigation/sight rules remain independent of material transparency, so the mistress cannot see or reach through the privacy walls. The generator and level module keep their existing `mirror-maze-*` filenames for compatibility.

## Creative brief

Shidan is a beloved black pet rabbit with a small white nose marking and white feet. The player inhabits her world from a rabbit-height third-person perspective. She likes rabbit food, vegetables, fruit, clean water, and chewing cardboard, books and wires. The tone is playful and affectionate, with the readable silhouettes and light colors of a cozy cartoon/anime world. Eden Eternal is a visual mood reference; no assets from it are copied.

The full game is a score chase before the masters go to sleep. Shidan begins in her circular, open-topped daytime den. She must use a long jump to escape, find happiness in the house, and avoid being caught and returned to the den. At bedtime the cotton cover ends the round. The player enters a name and their final score appears on a leaderboard.

## Milestone 1 scope

Milestone 1 established the home, Shidan and movement before adding pressure or punishment. That release had no timer, enemy, feeding system or final score; decorative food and books indicated future interaction locations. Three initial prompts introduce escape, stairs and the balcony; seven optional discoveries encourage furniture climbing and route exploration without a timer. Milestone 2 now adds the energy and consumable system described below.

### Shidan’s model

The three user photographs in `pic/` were inspected locally. Defining details are the compact black body, long upright ears, dark eyes, small white nose patch, white toes and dark tail. The original model uses procedural meshes and a simple part hierarchy, so it can be adjusted directly in code without Blender. Animation is procedural: breathing, blinking, small ear movements, synchronized paws, crouch and jump poses.

This is a stylized first-pass model, not a final skinned character mesh. Ear tips are excluded from the compact body collider to avoid snagging on every surface.

`rabbit-clearance.js` supplies stateless headroom for the rendered ear/body tuck without changing physics or jump strength. Authored wall segments and upright window panes carry `verticalBarrier` metadata. These side barriers affect clearance only where they overlap Shidan's actual circular body footprint, so running beside a window keeps her ordinary pose. Horizontal roofs and furniture undersides retain the wider visual clearance query; window lintels remain relevant while she crosses beneath them. The model restores its ordinary pose directly from current clearance, with no added hysteresis. Regression checks cover real window-side movement, charged jumps through an open window, low tunnels and exact pose restoration.

### Movement

WASD moves relative to the independently orbiting camera. Shift raises target speed; acceleration and stopping depend on the surface. Pressing Space starts a crouch, releasing it jumps. A very short press produces a hop; holding for roughly 0.65 seconds reaches full charge. A charged jump on bedding, carpet, grass, the balcony or basement concrete clears the fence height. Every long jump launched inside the den costs zero energy, including repeated jumps, stationary jumps and jumps toward its centre. This remains available at empty energy without a usage limit or reset requirement. Charged jumps outside the den cost the normal 5–10 energy. On wood/stairs the same input produces only a small hop.

Current tuning, intended for playtesting:

| Surface | Walk m/s | Run m/s | Acceleration m/s² | Long jump |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Bedding | 3.64 | 7.8 | 26 | Yes |
| Carpet | 3.9 | 8.5 | 28 | Yes |
| Indoor wood | 3.9 | 8.2 | 9 | No |
| Stairs | 3.64 | 7 | 24 | No |
| Grass / outdoor deck / concrete | 3.9 | 8.8 | Immediate grip | Yes |

Indoor wood retains a moderate slide; its drag is 4.5 s⁻¹, and acceleration is 9 m/s². Releasing movement slides about 0.85 m from walking speed or 1.8 m from full sprint, between the original and the very grippy revision. Outdoor ground, balcony and basement concrete surfaces stop immediately, including outdoor benches and stepping stones. Airborne steering preserves sprint speed through a normal hop.

Gravity is 16 m/s², normal jump impulse 4.2 m/s, fully charged impulse 8.25 m/s. Charged jumps recover forward momentum after brushing the fence, making escape work both from the center and beside the fence. These exaggerated arcade values prioritize reliable den escape. Small hops recover forward movement after brushing a ledge during takeoff, so the rabbit can clear a 0.42 m sill even from a stop. Their jump height remains unchanged; indoor wood keeps moderate sliding with much faster acceleration. Charged jumps and moving sprints use energy as described below. A small step allowance makes the stair-to-landing transition reliable. Simulation substeps prevent tunnelling at low frame rates.

### Poop balls and carried supply

Right-click the game, or press P on a trackpad, to spend one carried ball and drop a 0.065 m radius brown ball behind Shidan. Each day starts with ten carried balls, with a maximum of twenty and a one-second active-play cooldown between drops. Empty supply and cooldown prevent a drop without spending anything. Balls collide with the room, furniture, ceilings, den fence, stairs, other balls and Shidan's heavier capsule; Shidan can push them. They remain through pause, return-home and title navigation. The map holds 1,000 dropped balls, replacing only the oldest when a new drop would exceed the cap. Bedtime or reloading starts a new session; disk persistence is not part of this prototype.

`poop-supply.js` separates carried supply from the sphere pool. Fully consumed carrots, books, apples, rabbit food, bananas and bread grant two balls; ordinary hay grants three; noodles and cake grant five. Other finite items grant none. All grants stop at twenty. Special den hay grants three once per den visit, including the starting visit: actual travel outside the den followed by landing inside renews the allowance, while jumping vertically or repeated eating does not. The visit's reward is spent even when carried supply is already full. This gate affects only the ball reward; permanent hay's four-second full-energy refill remains repeatable.

Hold left-click near a dropped ball for one uninterrupted second to consume it. Completion removes that exact physical/rendered ball and grants five energy over the normal recovery period, with no happiness or carried-ball reward. Ball eating uses grounded reach and obstruction checks, cannot transfer progress between targets, and does not enter the regular-snack count or restock pool.

The balls use a fixed-capacity instance pool, spatial collision grids, and sleeping bodies that wake on contact. Sleeping preserves collision and rendering. They are rendered in one instanced draw instead of creating 1,000 independent meshes. The sphere solver is intentionally separate from the rabbit's kinematic controller. Removing an eaten ball preserves other live ball identities and permits later reuse of the freed capacity.

## Milestone 2 — energy and consumables (0.3.1)

The requested second milestone adds an energy loop to the exploration foundation. `energy.js` owns the 100-point meter, charge-based 5–10 point jump cost, continuous passive recovery at 0.01 points per 0.5 seconds (0.02 points/second, or 0.002 per 0.1-second frame), and food recovery spread over one second. The controller exempts every long jump launched inside the den from the energy cost, regardless of movement direction or prior jumps. Sprint drain includes passive recovery, giving exactly 30 seconds from full to empty while continuously sprinting. The controller charges only moving sprint input, not standing/blocked Shift or crouching. At exhaustion it returns to ordinary movement and requires Shift release before another sprint; unaffordable charged leaps outside the den become free small hops. Existing surface-specific jump restrictions remain.

`consumable-data.js` defines the original nine snack kinds and 47 authored starting placements outside the den, plus the later chewables and poop-ball rewards described below. The shared spawn pool excludes the den plus 0.4 m of clearance, keeping both initial items and restocks away from its fence. `consumable-system.js` keeps the simulation independent of rendering: reward-scaled uninterrupted holds, nearby grounded reach, wall/floor/den-fence occlusion, removal, and restock timers. Hold progress never transfers between items. Releasing, leaving reach, or opening a menu cancels it. One press consumes one item even at full energy.

Permanent special hay is a separate recovery point inside the den, reachable from the reset position. A four-second uninterrupted hold refills energy immediately to exactly 100 and clears pending food recovery. This follows the consumption formula using a 100-energy reward. Interrupted holds grant nothing. The hay requires grounded reach inside the den and obeys obstruction checks; it never disappears, never enters the finite snack count or random pool, and requires a fresh press for each use. It provides full recovery before exploring after returning home.

The current regular-snack pool contains eight kinds and starts with 45 items; the old library book slot now lies in the basement stair opening and is excluded. While fewer than two regular snacks remain, each 60 seconds of active play adds one. The kind is sampled with weight `1 / energy`, before choosing a vacant place associated with that kind. At two regular snacks remaining the timer stops and resets. Cake is excluded from this count and pool: the single cake returns to its reserved original location 120 seconds after full consumption, including when it was pushed elsewhere before being eaten. Ordinary hay has increased from six to twelve starting stacks, adding one in the bedroom, reading room, study, balcony, front yard and backyard. Their energy, happiness, carried-poop rewards and consume time are unchanged. Coordinates are listed in LEVEL.md. Counts and exact rewards are listed in README.md. Pauses, maps, tours, title, and hidden tabs freeze resource time; home/title navigation retains energy, pending rewards and remaining items until bedtime or page reload.

`consumables.js` gives each kind original procedural geometry. Hay is a cylindrical sheaf of fine, bent grass blades with uneven tips and a thin twine tie. The larger special bundle has a cream dish and mint bow, merged into one extra drawing batch. Each finite kind/floor shares an instanced mesh, limiting all food objects to 19 drawing submissions plus a nearby-item ring and particle batch. Selected items wiggle during eating. Finite snacks then lift, shrink, and release golden particles, becoming unavailable immediately; their visual effect lasts 0.65 seconds. The permanent stack releases particles while staying visible. Neither consumables nor particles cast into the cached sunlight map. The upper collectible layer follows the house cutaway.

Happiness, cardboard/wire interactions, drinking, gameplay noise and NPC responses to noise are implemented in the extensions below.

## Daylight and bedtime (0.4.0)

`day-cycle.js` owns a 600-second active-play clock, independent of the capped physics step. It settles elapsed time whenever play is paused or resumed, preventing pause intervals from being charged on the next frame. Pause, title, map, house view and hidden-tab states stop it; returning to the den with R/Home does not restart it. Full night starts at 480 seconds elapsed, leaving 120 seconds of lamplit exploration. The upper-right HUD shows time until bedtime, the current phase and happiness. The original Clover House header banner and curiosity panel have been removed. Energy and bladder meters sit on the left, with carried poop supply below. Poop and pee cooldowns display decimal seconds, with a progress bar during the pee action. The map/discoveries button is attached to the minimap, remaining visible in compact form on narrower or shorter screens.

`day-lighting.js` smoothly blends blue noon, warm dusk and cool night sky/ambient/direct lighting, with stars and a moon fading in. The sun direction remains fixed to reuse the cached sunlight shadow map. Twenty-four authored ceiling/floor fixtures have physical collision. The eighteen above-ground fixtures retain their two diffuser batches and shared day/night emissive material; six basement ceiling lamps use an independent third batch. Their baseline power is 22% at noon, warming and increasing to full power with the same dusk/night progression. Four constant, unshadowed point lights follow the nearest lamp locations on Shidan’s current floor, preserving shader variants and fading replacements rather than switching on camera turns. This approximates local lighting without adding per-lamp shadow passes. The sky is one low-detail gradient dome plus star and moon draws.

At expiry, the game clears held input, closes overlays and freezes in a Game Over dialog. `round-stats.js` snapshots actual completed consumptions, successful waste actions, water/urine transfers, jumps, sprint time, distance, captures and mistress cleanup. Final score and competition rank (one plus the number of strictly higher scores) use the full local leaderboard, including results outside its top-ten display. Results remain frozen until the player chooses a new game or the main menu. Those choices reset the ended round; each completed score is recorded once. `adventure-session.js` resets energy/recovery, snacks/restock/visual effects, the ball pool/colliders, carried supply, den-reward eligibility, bladder, drinking cooldown and puddles; discoveries, camera and clock also reset. The house and renderer are reused. Manual title navigation during play continues to preserve the current round and its statistics. `start.js` supplies normal real time; the development-only daylight preview injects a controlled clock to verify the same game code.

The Escape menu's **Restart game** command uses a complete fresh-round reset, rather than the old return-to-den action. It abandons the partial score without saving it, retains the same player name and completed leaderboard, and restores noon/600 seconds, zero happiness, full energy, ten carried balls, an empty bladder, full fountain reserves and authored items. Dropped balls, puddles, discoveries, pending recovery, interactions and cooldowns are cleared. The existing R/Home action still returns Shidan to her den while preserving the active round.

Happiness points and the local leaderboard are implemented below. A target score and cotton-cover animation remain future work.

## Happiness and chewables (0.5.0)

`happiness.js` owns named rounds and local completed-score persistence. Completed consume events and the one-time completed blue-plush interaction add points. A round keeps its name and score through pause, home and manual title navigation. At bedtime it saves exactly once before resource reset, including zero scores; new rounds start at zero. The board keeps all valid runs and displays the highest ten, with earlier finish times breaking ties. Names are normalized and limited to 24 characters, then rendered with textContent. Invalid saved rows are discarded; storage failures retain session results and show a clear message. Browser QA injects isolated storage to avoid test rows in real scores.

Food rewards (energy / happiness) are carrot 20 / 10, ordinary hay 40 / 0, apple 25 / 50, rabbit food 20 / 80, banana 20 / 40, bread 30 / 20, noodles 30 / 10 and cake 50 / 150. Books give 10 energy and 20 happiness. Cake takes five seconds to consume and has its own 120-second respawn at its original reserved location, with at most one available. The other eight regular snack kinds retain the shared below-two restocking rule; cake neither participates in nor suppresses that pool. Wire = 150 happiness / 0 energy / 30-second cooldown; cardboard = 100 happiness / 20 energy / 60-second cooldown; slippers = 200 happiness / 0 energy / 90-second cooldown. Consumption time is `max(1, E / 25 + H / 50)` seconds, rounded up to 0.1 second, using listed energy and happiness rewards. Carrot and book take 1 second; apple 2; rabbit food 2.4; ordinary hay, banana and bread 1.6; noodles 1.4; cake 5; wire 3; cardboard 2.8; and slippers 4. Permanent den hay uses E=100 and H=0, taking four seconds before its instant refill to 100%. It gives no happiness. Cooldowns start after full consumption, use the active-play clock and pause with menus. Each consumed item with a cooldown has an independent replacement deadline.

Ten wire coils begin beside authored appliance/lamp locations. Eight boxes and four pairs of slippers choose random collision-checked floor locations across the above-ground level, outside the den. Basement and basement-stair candidates are excluded from household authored items, random placements and respawns; the magical scene owns a separate one-use supply pool. The default furnished-world consumable system and production game both begin with 46 finite snacks from 47 authored slots; the old library stair slot is filtered. Candidate places are cached once; replacement selection excludes occupied/reserved snack places and the player. All consumables are physical, movable bodies. Shidan pushes them with about 10% contact slowdown, increased to 18% for boxes, so they yield instead of acting as immovable walls. Momentum, friction and gravity carry or settle the props, including falling off tables. Bodies collide with scenery, den fencing, lot boundaries, other loose props and poop balls. Permanent hay can be nudged inside the den, with fence constraints keeping the reusable refill point there.

Finite items remove their bodies only on full consumption; household items restore bodies on their normal respawn schedule, while maze bonus items never respawn. Permanent hay retains its body after use. Pushed cake and wires respawn at their original authored locations, not at their consumed coordinates. The rabbit and camera distinguish loose consumables from permanent scenery; loose props are not climbable support platforms, while structural furniture and book steps keep their existing climbing collision. Prop and poop interactions wake nearby sleeping bodies as needed. Reset replaces item bodies without rebuilding house geometry or the renderer.

Original procedural models are instanced by kind/floor, adding at most six drawing submissions to the prior food system (25 total possible collectible batches). New TV/oven/computer details are merged with static scenery. Dynamic collectibles never alter the cached sun shadow map.

The right-side **Item rewards** board is a semantic table built once by `consumable-legend.js` from the same reward definitions as gameplay. Its fifteen rows cover the twelve ordinary item kinds, special den hay, dropped poop and water. Energy and happiness are separate columns; special hay's 100% means a full energy refill, and water has no energy/happiness reward. A native details heading allows pointer and keyboard collapse. The board opens by default and scrolls within the space between the clock and minimap. The minimap is a separate fixed lower-right panel, visible whether the legend is open or closed, with its map/discoveries button retained at compact sizes.

## Water, bladder and pee

Drinking grants no capture immunity. Static fountainId collider groups define each bowl's 1.15 m interaction zone on its own floor. The mistress chooses the closest valid human standing point and uses a local 1.2 m hand reach instead of stopping just beyond her ordinary 0.8 m capture radius. Existing physical hand rays still enforce walls, glass and other solid barriers. The ordinary caught event cancels the water hold and stops transfers; only the separate full one-second pee action remains protected.

Eighteen fixed fountains cover the level: fourteen above ground across seven ground-floor areas, four upper rooms, balcony, front yard and backyard, plus four in the frosted-glass maze. Maze fountain sources follow the current scene while keeping independent reserves and do not alter household supply counts. Fountains leave doors, stair landings and maze connections clear. Their bases have fixed collision and cannot be pushed. Each independently holds 100 bladder percentage points at the start of a round. `water.js` owns authored positions, grounded reach, obstruction checks and remaining reserves. `fountains.js` supplies original procedural fountain models and puddle rendering.

Hold left-click near a fountain to drink continuously while Shidan performs a nibbling motion. Starting at zero, bladder value rises by exactly ten percentage points per second of drinking, so 0.1 second adds one point. Deduct exactly the accepted bladder gain from that fountain's reserve, limiting it by bladder headroom and available water. Partial drinking leaves the unused reserve available for later; this is capacity-based depletion, not a one-use interaction. The simulation retains fractional progress without rounding to display precision; the HUD displays two decimals. Filling stops at 100% or an empty reserve, and leaving reach, release or a menu stops drinking. The prompt shows remaining water or that the fountain is empty until the next round. Water gives no energy, happiness or carried balls. Food and fountain interactions share the eat/drink prompt while keeping their distinct hold behavior.

A held fountain request uses a separate pending state while drinking is temporarily unavailable. It starts automatically at the cooldown deadline, crediting only the eligible part of a crossing frame. Stop, menus, capture and leaving reach clear it. Pending holds have no drinking animation or water transfer until eligible, and a completed full-bladder hold ends normally. Round water statistics count every positive transfer, including the pending-to-active transition.

Press Q only with a 100% full bladder and no cooldown to begin a one-second pee animation, gradually draining the bladder to zero and leaving a puddle on the static supporting surface beneath Shidan. The action locks movement, jumping, eating, drinking and pooping until complete. Shidan remains visible to perception throughout, but all capture checks are disabled for the full one-second action. When it finishes, the completed puddle retains cleanup priority and takes 15 seconds to mop after arrival. The simulation settles NPC time before advancing the water action and splits the exact completion boundary, preventing the action time from being borrowed for mopping or an early catch. A sixty-second active-play cooldown starts at action onset, blocking another pee or drink; Q presses below 100% or during cooldown do nothing, preserve the bladder and do not start or extend the restriction. Action progress and larger, semibold decimal-seconds readiness appear beside the bladder meter, with a matching carried-poop timer. Actions and cooldowns pause with the round; R/Home and manual title navigation preserve bladder, fountain reserves and cooldown state. Bedtime or Restart game clears these states and refills all fountains to 100. Puddles are visual floor patches with depth separation, rather than obstacles or new movement penalties.

## Gameplay noise and future hearing

The noise backend exposes queryable gameplay events for future NPC responses. An actual long-jump launch emits nearby noise, and moving sprint actions continue emitting while active. Ordinary walking footsteps are silent in both audio playback and gameplay hearing; footstep sounds accompany only actual moving sprints. Long-jump and sprint noise use a five-metre spherical hearing radius, measured with full three-dimensional distance from the emitted position. Short tap jumps, charged inputs that produce only a small hop and holding Shift while stationary emit no gameplay noise. Long jumps launched inside the den remain noisy despite costing no energy.

A valid in-progress eating/chewing hold emits globally audible noise whenever the item's listed happiness reward is positive. Use the reward definition, rather than successful completion or current score, to decide: cancelling a hold before its reward is awarded does not undo already emitted events. Rewarding Shidan chewing reaches listeners anywhere on the map: all three stairs and their landings, the basement, balcony, rooms and yards. It has no visibility, house-boundary or distance check. The stream retains its legacy house scope label for ripple and diagnostic compatibility; canHearNoise recognizes rewarding Shidan eat events globally. Household ambience retains its indoor scope, and movement sounds retain their 5 m sphere. The pursuit layer still applies the den and protected-source rules. Ordinary hay, special den hay, dropped poop balls and drinking remain silent because they give zero happiness.

Subtle expanding ripples communicate noise without adding a new HUD panel. Stopping a sprint or ending a consume hold stops future emissions; current ripples finish their fade. Menus freeze noise progression with active-play time. Bedtime and Restart game clear noise events and ripples with the rest of the round. Hearing queries remain independent of optional Web Audio output: toggling Sound off cannot make Shidan stealthier or change future AI detection.

`noise.js` implements the graphics-independent `NoiseSystem`: `update` advances active actions, `events` retains pulses with immutable source positions, `activeKinds` describes current sources, and `audibleTo(listener)` queries hearing. The exported `canHearNoise` applies the same scope/distance rules to an individual event. Moving sprints pulse every 0.55 seconds and positive-happiness holds every 0.75 seconds; a long jump pulses once on takeoff. Events expire after 1.2 seconds of active play. `noise-ripples.js` exposes `createNoiseRipples`, drawing the feedback in one instanced submission.

The household routine emits its own noise. The pursuit layer consumes Shidan's stream, with noise masking and safe-area rules described below.

## Mistress household routine

## Detection, pursuit and capture

`mistress-vision.js` supplies the same horizontal 90° forward cone with a 50 m three-dimensional range during every awake phase, including household routines and alert searching. Eye rays target cached world-space samples from Shidan's actual animated body, head and ears, so a visible head or ear above furniture can reveal her when all lower samples are hidden. Sight normally refreshes every 0.1 seconds; phase changes, entry onto or exit from a tabletop, and turns greater than 0.12 radians force an immediate refresh. Physical capture retains its separate body and hand-ray checks. Physical walls remain occluders even while a player-camera cutaway hides their render groups. Transparent glass permits sight but physical solid segments block capture through it. Sleeping disables vision; audible noise can still interrupt sleep. The den suppresses both detection types. Other inaccessible areas suppress hearing and capture, but a visibly exposed rabbit can remain noticed at their entrance until she actually breaks sight.

`MistressPursuit` wraps the routine with routine/chase/search/caught/scolding/returning phases. Only new, live Shidan events are heard, using their own clock. Every rejected event ID is consumed, including masked/safe/cooldown events, so it cannot alert later. Long-jump noise snapshots the launch point before physics moves Shidan. Live household activity noise masks hearing while sight remains active. NPC footsteps do not mask hearing.

Chase speed is the current rabbit surface's configured sprint speed multiplied by 0.60, 0.65, 0.70, 0.75 or 0.80 at elapsed times 0, 120, 240, 360 and 480 seconds. Movement follows clear cached segments and replans at most every 0.35 seconds. Turns of at least 40° brake before the corner and pause for 0.25 seconds, including the diagonal turns used by real doorway routes. Handled-corner records expire after moving two metres away so later loops still brake. More than two consecutive seconds without confirmed sight switches to walking/searching, even if noises were heard during that chase. Entering protected cover and breaking sight switches immediately.

Search walks among random reachable points around the last known location for 10–20 seconds. Renewed sight or a fresh audible noise starts pursuit again. Sprint events alone are ignored for two seconds after the initial slowdown; this grace period is implicit in play. If nothing is reacquired, she walks back to her couch and resumes her phone routine. She can pursue through exterior doors, either yard, the balcony and all three stairways, including into the basement.

A fresh accepted sound without current sight creates an investigation of that immutable source position. She runs along a human-clear route to it without starting the two-second visual-loss timer. Arrival without seeing Shidan starts the usual random search; subsequent sounds replace the destination, while confirmed sight switches back to visual pursuit. The destination does not follow the unseen rabbit or change when she enters protected cover elsewhere. Inaccessible noise origins remain suppressed. The HUD distinguishes investigating a sound from a visual chase. ## Technical decisions

- Three.js 0.180.0, pinned locally: instant browser play, low setup overhead, no proprietary editor or asset pipeline.
- Plain ES modules and Node built-ins: no package-install step or runtime CDN dependence.
- Custom kinematic rabbit controller, a sphere solver for poop balls, and small movable consumable bodies with momentum, friction, gravity and contact slowdown. Structural furniture stays static and climbable; general-purpose dynamic furniture remains a later engine decision.
- Static geometry uses indexed, vertex-coloured batches in 8 m cells, separately for ground, upper and outdoor layers. The camera can reject off-screen cells; compatible pastel colours share materials. Rigid details within each rabbit joint are batched while the joints remain independently animated (11 draws including the contact shadow).
- Shaders and all room geometry buffers are prepared before enabling Play. The 3D drawing buffer is capped at 2.07 million pixels / 1.5× pixel ratio; the HTML interface remains native-resolution. HUD panels use a cream fill instead of live backdrop blur, and unchanged HUD text is not rewritten. Paused, hidden and map-covered scenes stop rendering after one final frame.
- Cached sunlight shadows, local collision queries and sleeping balls remain in place. Ball matrix uploads cover only changed slots, with adjacent ranges combined by Three.js.
- `/tests/performance.html` measures actual camera orbit frame, CPU and asynchronous GPU times. `node scripts/benchmark-render.mjs` compares draw/triangle workload across six locations without a GPU. Neither tool runs in normal play; per-frame GPU profiling requires `?profile=1`.
- A local Node server binds only to 127.0.0.1 and serves an explicit allowlist. Personal reference photos never enter the web build.
- Camera casts against collision boxes to shorten its orbit near furniture and floors. The wire den is excluded from camera collision so the rabbit remains visible from outside the fence.
- Original synthesized effects are enabled by default and unlock on the first user gesture; the Sound toggle can mute them. Background music is deferred until a suitable track is selected.
- Gameplay noise and hearing queries run independently from sound playback. Distance checks include height; happiness-positive consume holds are audible anywhere on the map through walls and floors. Menus pause this state and a fresh round clears it.

## Surface stability

The house uses actual clearance or clean butt joins between different coloured solids: window/door frames, sills, wall trim, sofa bases/arms, chair backs, crate sides, balcony rails, roof/ceiling junctions, lamp beams and book binding ends. Intentional floor overlays use cached polygon-offset materials while retaining normal depth testing/writing. Normal materials are never globally offset. Thin upstairs floor colours have an 8 mm reveal; the camera near plane varies from 0.06 m only in a squeezed chase view to 0.18 m at normal distances and 0.75 m in the survey, improving depth precision without losing close camera clearance.

The test-only surface audit transforms real mesh triangles, including instances and vertex colours after batching, and clips their projected overlap. It flags positive-area, differently coloured coplanar patches with competing facing/depth state. It ignores shared edges and true crossing shapes. Exposure checks use actual opaque box meshes plus the legal camera envelope, so buried internal faces do not create false failures. Tests cover the full house before/after batching, all consumables, animated rabbit poses, and actual pushing of neighbouring books; reproductions of old defects ensure the checker can detect regressions. No geometric audit runs during gameplay.
